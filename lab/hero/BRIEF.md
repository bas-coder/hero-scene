# Hero scene brief (read fully before changing the scene)

The hero is one persistent world: a desk at night. It is **black by default** (unlit, empty). Each project is a **shot**: it brings its own **dressing** (material colours from its reference) and its own **mood** (lights + grade). The intro's `sun` channel brings the world from black to alive. Shot 01 is Flable; its reference is `Showcasing/Flable AI/sample 1.jpg` (a reference only, never shipped).

**Bar:** a still from a AAA game, not a WebGL demo. A score of 8/10 or better on every element from two blind judges (the lower score counts). This bar must hold at **DPR 1** (Abbas's screen, Intel HD 4000) as well as at retina.

## Files

```
lab/hero/index.html             the lab: the real engine outside Next (port 4192, `hero-lab` launch config)
lab/hero/hud.json               the live site's HUD boxes (scripts/shot.mjs --hud)
src/hero/engine/                the scene (plain three.js, shared by the lab and the site; index.d.ts types it)
src/hero/engine/palette.js      world (black default), dressings and moods per shot: token names + numbers
src/design/primitives.css       --color-hero-* (the colour values)  →  theme.css --scene-hero-* (the aliases)
src/components/HeroCanvas.tsx   the site's wrapper: tokens, the page's clocks, live HUD, capture/QA bridges
scripts/build-plates.mjs        each shot's room plate (assets/hero/plates → public/media/hero/<shot>)
scripts/serve-lab.mjs           static server     scripts/shot.mjs   headless captures, bench, --probe
```

**Colours live only in the design collections.** To change one, edit its `--color-hero-*` primitive; the lab and the site both read it (the lab loads the site's CSS). Numbers (intensities, grade, roughness) are in `palette.js`.

Lab params: `?shot=flable ?mood=base|flable ?dressing=none|flable ?q=low|medium|high ?hud=0`, held frames `?intro= ?t= ?exit=`, `&bench=180`, isolation `?hide=<object names>`. The site holds frames the same way: `/?capture&t=7.8` (`&hud-qa` for the HUD checks).

## Taste rules (a broken rule caps that element at 6)

**Composition and medium**
1. Medium per element: code for regular geometry (desk, laptop rig, cups, pencils, lamp, notebook, phone); generated plates for the out-of-focus room (wall, shelf, bed, plant, curtain); the real UI images on the screen; DOM for any text. No people in code. No HTML drawings of objects.
2. No text inside the 3D unless it is a physical object.
3. Specific, not generic: every object reads as that object at full size. Clichés to avoid: floating devices in a void; neon glow on everything; colour painted onto surfaces instead of coming from light.

**World, dressing, mood**
4. The default world is black. A shot dresses it from its reference and lights it with its mood. Hue comes from motivated sources: lamp, window, practicals, screen.
5. Everything a shot adds (light, environment, plates, grade lift) scales with `sun`, so the unlit world is truly black.
6. Shadows keep depth. Flable's shadows are deep violet (as in the reference), never a uniform haze that lifts everything.

**Materials**
7. Real at DPR 1 and 2: bevels on every box, roughness that varies, contact shadows. Nothing floats or sinks into the desk.
8. No faceted normals, no tiled repeats, no uniform grunge.
9. Metal reflects bright things: the environment is built from emissive cards in the mood's colours.
10. Fine detail fades with screen footprint, or it shimmers.

**Light and post**
11. One motivated key (the lamp), the screen spill, the practicals; ambient stays low.
12. HalfFloat target; tone-map once (ACES) in post.js. Bloom only on genuinely bright things.
13. No decorative shines: no sweeps, streaks or sheen wipes. Post never hides weak materials.
14. Dither after encoding (dark gradients band).

**Screen and UI**
15. The screen shows the real UI images. It is excluded from tone mapping and grade (alpha 0 in the HDR target), so it stays crisp and exact. It is never warped.
16. Legible at DPR 1.

**Motion**
17. The timeline is the old flable.ts, verbatim. Nothing decorative is added. Ambient motion stays subtle. **Amended (Abbas, 19 Oct):** one beat added in the Chats window, the prompt box lifting out (the dim from 0.5 s, the lift 1.15–4 s, the light open by 4.4 s); every other key is unchanged.

**Learned in review (Gate B)**
18. Sample the screen with a mip bias, never at a fixed LOD 0: LOD 0 kills the mips and the UI blurs and aliases at once.
19. Environment cards sit at the real lights' positions. A card with no source in the frame shows up as a phantom glint on the metal.
20. Grain goes after sRGB encoding, luma only, and fades out in the blacks; before encoding it turns coarse and lifts the black.
21. The intro is staged: the screen wakes, then the lamp ignites (bulb and pool together), then the room. The grade's lift scales with the room's light, so the unlit world stays black.
22. A lamp hood shows its diffuser as a crescent under the rim (about 16° toward the camera). A full disc reads as a plate.
23. Floors seen at a grazing angle: cut the specular and bake the falloff (map + aoMap), or the practical's sheen flattens them into one bright sheet.
24. A feature that is not built yet never leaves a hole: the breakout socket stays closed until its tiles exist.
25. Props keep real proportions and stay fully in frame at every loop pose: pencils stand about 0.6× the cup height above the rim; the lamp's arm never leaves the top of the frame.
26. Put the key where the reference puts it. The lamp's arm reaches forward, so the pool lands on the desk's front and lights the cups' faces. A key behind the props leaves every camera-facing face dead.
27. Room bounce comes through the environment (a dim card for the room behind the viewer), never a flat hemisphere fill. A directional fill lifts the faces turned to the camera and keeps the shadows deep.
28. Screen spill is an area light the size of the display, riding on the lid. A point light leaves a hot glint on the hinge.
29. The bloom threshold sits above every lit surface; only emitters (the diffuser) cross it.
30. Each prop carries its one telling detail: the phone lies face down with its camera plateau; the notebook has its spine, board edges and elastic band; pencils have faceted barrels, a wood cone and a graphite point.
31. Judge the intro on every stage (0.4, 0.75, 1.1, 1.6, 2.2 s), not just its ends.
32. Shader budget: every distinct material feature set compiles its own program, and on a weak GPU that dominates the time to first frame. Props use MeshStandardMaterial and share programs, and only the desk keeps a clearcoat. Programs compile with `compileAsync` against the HDR target before the first frame. Check `lab.timing` after any material change (14 Oct: 23 programs, about 10 s to first frame headless on the HD 4000).
33. The grade's lift must stay a hint (0.0016 at most in a channel): 0.012 lifted every black to 25/255 blue, and the judges read that as violet painted onto the shadows.
34. Bloom sums every level (the up pass adds onto each finer one), so emitters keep a tight core; the widest blur alone wraps whole objects in haze.
35. Ground every object with a soft contact shadow; the lamp's shadow map is too coarse to seat them.
36. A light whose source isn't visible in the frame reads as "a rim from nowhere". Keep a practical faint until its source (the shelf's plate) is in the shot, and never at a grazing angle to a glossy surface: the desk mirrors it as a glint.
37. The room's bounce is muted (a desaturated mauve, not the window's violet). A saturated bounce turns every face toward the camera electric indigo.
38. Keep long props across the frame, not toward the camera: an arm reaching straight at the lens foreshortens into a stub.
39. Any texture an emitter multiplies into HDR (the diffuser) is half-float; 8-bit steps × 28 show as rings.
40. **The intro starts from black (Abbas, 14 Oct).** The Drawn to Real paper is the world's void, not a shot colour; the shot's colour arrives with its ink, its lit things and then its real image. No dull purple before the shot comes in.
41. **The vignette falls off to dark (Abbas, 14 Oct):** a lens falloff to near black at the corners, not a light tint over a bright plate.
42. Ink draws geometry silhouettes only: an edge must hold at 1 px and at 3 px (flecks, leather and legends don't), and the screen and the plate take a graphite wash, never scribbles. Light sources glow through the drawing.
43. Transitions are soft and low-frequency: the fill sweep wobbles like a hand; it never shows block noise or grain.
44. A shot's room plate is graded to its reference by measurement (wall, LED band, under the desk), dithered with an unstructured hash, and never padded by copying or mirroring its edges into view.
45. Lathe profiles run bottom to top. Top to bottom winds the lathe inside out: the camera sees its open underside, which z-fights anything lying at that height (the lamp foot's "blinking shadow" was its underside against the contact shadow, 15 Oct). Probe the normals after building one. The hood stays inside out on purpose (world.js says why).
46. Nothing thinner than a pixel may catch light at 1x. A lit sliver under a pixel tall dashes, because the samples catch it in runs (the lid's 0.7 mm aluminium lip above the glass, 15 Oct). Give it its dark neighbour's material, or make it at least a pixel wide.
47. **The headline's side stays dim (Abbas, 15 Oct).** The laptop's left base, the desk in front of it, the notebook and the phone sit at about the phone and book's level (sRGB luma at 1440×900, t 1.8: palm rest about 150, the desk band in front of the laptop about 120–140, notebook about 65–80; the reference is darker still). The screen reaches them twice, through its area light and the environment's screen card, so both stay low. Materials answer the screen's area light per material (`screenResponse`, mood.js): polished aluminium keeps 30% of its specular (`laptop.screenSheen`), and the desk keeps 30% of its spill (`deskScreenSpill`; it only sees the screen at a grazing angle, where a real panel emits far less). The desk top carries a baked falloff away from the lamp, darker toward the left and along its front edge and front face (the band that rises into the headline when the camera zooms).
48. **The desk stands on a frame (Abbas, 15 Oct):** slim satin-grey steel legs on glides, a 3.5 cm apron set 5 cm back from the top's edges, low stretchers. The frame is narrower than the top, so both front legs stay in view on desktop through the loop. It takes less of the environment and no lamp light (the top casts a shadow), and its paint fades toward the floor (vertex colours), where soft wide shadows ground the feet and the frame, so it stays dim behind the headline. Never the same white as the top: one material reads as a blockout.
49. Whatever the shot draws over the UI follows the UI's own geometry (`shot.ui`). The spotlight takes the corner radius of the card it lands on (a `focusR` channel: the KPI card 20 px, the stepper's card about 12 px on the 2048 screens). The lifted cards and their shadows take the UI cards' radius on every corner. The cards are the crispest, most neutral white in frame: they sample a native-resolution crop of the strip (`breakout.detail`, built by `scripts/build-images.mjs`), with no lip or halo (a coloured lip reads as a foot strip). Each card carries its own soft drop shadow just behind it, deepening and dropping as it lifts and when hovered. Cast onto the far glass, the shadows drifted off the cards and read as a row of smudges. The slot the strip leaves takes the UI's sidebar grey, dimmed with the rest of the screen, never a black hole. The dim is a deep, nearly neutral veil, so the rest of the UI recedes rather than fogging violet. Fanned, the arc stays on the glass it left.
50. The floating cards answer the cursor with springs: the hovered card lifts straight toward the camera and rises a little (so it grows in place, never drifts into its neighbour), about 4% larger, up to 8° of tilt toward the cursor, with a deeper drop shadow and a brighter rim, while the neighbours ease back and a little apart. Fine pointers with full motion only. No new timeline keys and no light sweeps (rule 13).
51. Devices are the current models, built in code to read at 1x. The phone is an iPhone 17 Pro face down, turned about 30° so its long side and the plateau's step show in profile (pointing away from the camera it foreshortens to a slab), and clear of the notebook's band. It has the full-width plateau flush with the frame and three large lenses (deep black glass in silver rims, about 1.7 mm proud). The flash and LiDAR are ringed, the aluminium is satin, and the USB-C port sits between speaker and mic holes. It has no side keys (at 1x they read as notches) and no logo. The glass panel sits within a few percent of the aluminium's value: a lighter panel reads as a lit screen, which turns the phone face up. The phone sits on a tight contact shadow.
52. (Next, Abbas 15 Oct) Every other project gets its own camera path and device (Bricx-style: angles and devices vary per project, such as MacBook, iPhone or iPad). The shot spec carries both.
53. Shades laid over the room plate (contact shadows, the under-desk occlusion, the cards' shadows on the screen) never write alpha (`keepMask`, world.js). Alpha marks the ungraded path; a shade that wrote it pulled the plate under the desk into the graded world, whose saturation and gain clipped it to pure magenta right behind the headline.
54. A neutral prop keeps its read in a coloured light. Its dressing token is graded against the shot's light. Flable's silver phone carries a little green, because under the violet room it otherwise read as a purple phone, twice as saturated as the laptop. The laptop is lit white by its own screen.
55. The lit screen sits just under full brightness (0.92): a night scene's panel, not a clipped white card competing with the headline.
56. The room plate's band under the desk is a quiet violet near the reference's (lifted 1.35×, saturation 0.75; `scripts/build-plates.mjs`). At 1.8× the bed below the desk glowed magenta right behind the headline.
57. **The site frames like the lab (Abbas, 19 Oct).** The HUD is measured per text line (Range rects over the availability pill and each headline line, plus the action buttons). On desktop the lit screen may come down beside a short line (the pill, the name), never behind one, so on a short window the laptop keeps its 42%-of-width size, centred (the device may wander at most 12% of the width to buy size). The lab reads the same measurements from `hud.json`, re-measured from the live site at every size it previews (`scripts/shot.mjs --hud`, picked by nearest width and height). A stale or proportionally scaled HUD makes the lab lie.
58. A single-card breakout that travels (the Chats prompt box) comes toward the camera along the line of sight, face-on, and commits to its size: about 1.6× (the most that stays clear of the greeting's last line and covers the chips row whole; it never cuts through a line of text), then drifts right late in the lift (lift², so it reads as lifting, not widening) until its end hangs past the bezel into the clear wall, about 40 px short of the pencil cup at 1x (10% of it past the screen's edge, the bezel crossing between "Flash" and the send button; 9% on narrow layouts). Its drop shadow drops and softens further than the KPI cards' and ends at the lid's edge (a card a hand's width off the screen can't shade the far wall). A hover lifts it and deepens the shadow but grows it only about 1%, or it crowds its neighbours. It stays opaque until it is back in register (under a pixel off) and its slot refills under it only then: a card that travels must never fade in place, or it ghosts. The spotlight finishes dimming before the card moves and opens only after it lands.
59. Tooltips are frosted glass that visibly blurs what is behind (45% fill over a 20 px blur, `--tooltip-*`), with a near-opaque fallback where backdrop blur is unsupported. White tooltip text holds at least 4.5:1 inside the box (measured over the white screen and the dark keyboard).
